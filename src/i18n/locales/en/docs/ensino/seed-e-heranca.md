# Predictability, intervention and inheritance

**Audience:** Upper-secondary education, Biology and Physics. **Suggested time:** 45–50 min.
**Status:** technical draft; Biology and Physics teacher review pending.
**Starting lesson:** Same seed, one intervention (mesma-seed). Resources: WebGL
browser, paper or spreadsheet, and laboratory access. Follow the [common preparation](README.md).

## Objective

Separate computational reproduction, intervention effects and trait inheritance.
Distinguish observed divergence from proof of mathematical chaos.

## Steps and records

1. In 5 min, predict two futures from the same condition with an extra food portion
   in only one. Open **Same seed, one intervention**.
2. Record initial A/B states: B receives five extra units at one local position.
   Advance to day 3; record food, population and apparent trail positions.
3. Use **Replay scenario and interventions**, stop again at day 3 and compare the
   record. If you overshoot, repeat with a paused three-day advance. Equality
   requires identical time and interventions; rounding hides details.
4. Save a checkpoint and open **Inheritance and movement**. Father and other loci
   remain identical; the maternal locomotion locus changes from 0.2 to 0.8.
5. Select a worker with the same ID in A/B; record parentage, generation, mutations
   and distance. Advance one model day and compare distance increments.
6. In 10 min, conclude each part separately, distinguishing inheritance from
   environmental effects. Do not treat different seed numbers as identical
   replicates. Preserve records and restore the first lesson’s original backup.

## Discussion questions

- Does the same seed compensate for different interventions or times?
- Does divergence after extra food prove deterministic chaos?
- Does changing an allele guarantee more movement, success or adaptation?

## Annotated answer guidance

Reproduction requires identical inputs and schedule; extra food can change future
interactions without proving chaos. Distance includes pauses, tasks and detours:
it does not isolate intrinsic speed. Daughters combine maternal and paternal
inheritance and possible mutations. A numerical effect does not certify adaptive
advantage. A small or zero distance difference is compatible with the question;
discuss further controls or measurements needed.

## What the model does not represent

Genes are numerical vectors without real DNA; effects are uncalibrated. The recipe
controls only one maternal locus, not an entire life history. The simulator’s
forecast interval is exploratory, without statistically validated coverage for a
real terrarium.
